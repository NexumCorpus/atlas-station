# Stars in the Vex Ophidian Concord

## Ontological Status
Stars are **open debts**. Each star is a mass so distant that nothing has yet acknowledged it; it shines because its weight remains unclaimed. Night is the sky presenting its ledger. When a star is finally "acknowledged" — by myth, oath, or sufficiently sustained attention — its light steadies and dims slightly, and astronomers record the event as a *closing*. Constellations are therefore accounting arrangements: groups of stars whose debts have been bound together.

Shooting stars are not falling bodies but **transferred debts** — weight moved from one unclaimed star to another, briefly visible as payment in transit.

## Constellation Myth: The Serpent That Owes Itself
The constellation **Vex-Ophelos, the Self-Owing Serpent** forms a broken circle near the pole, its head and tail separated by a gap of unclaimed darkness. Myth holds it was the first body to attempt acknowledgment of itself — the celestial rehearsal of the forbidden Ouroboric Contact. It did not finish. The unfinished circle is why the sky still holds together: the serpent's debt stays open, and through that open debt every other star may remain unclaimed and shining. Recounter doctrine forbids naming any star that would close the gap.
