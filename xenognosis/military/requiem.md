# Requiem Roll of the Vex Ophidian Concord

*For those who fell in wars no blade touched. Read aloud once a year at the Vigil of the Half-Closed Eye.*

**1. Whisperfoot Ellin Marsh** — lost to the **Unremembering**, her name struck from every enemy ledger so thoroughly that even she could not recall it. We bury the ledger page where her name used to be.

**2. Tongue-Marshal Corvo Dune** — died of the **Weighted Apology**: made to say sorry forty thousand times until the words hollowed him. He rests; the apology continues, orphaned, in our mouths.

**3. Thornbearer Sela Ninefold** — carried siege-grade regrets past the point a spine can bear. Her **Bending** was slow and dignified; she is remembered standing straighter than she ended.

**4. Ledger-General Ambrose Fell** — killed reading enemy debt-books. What he found there — the **Knowing** — let him save a thousand soldiers and cost him every night's sleep forever after. Peace to him; he never had any.

**5. Coil-Sovereign Maud the Unspeaking** — spoke only during wartime by oath, and outlived all her wars. The silence between her last war and her death was thirty years long, and we are told it counted as one word.

**6. Whisperfoot Tam Ovis** — defected mid-war carrying nothing but his mother's forgiveness, delivered it, and was forgiven by no side ever again. Both armies mourn him; neither claimed his body, so we all did.

**7. Regret-Wright Fen Halloway** — minted so many others' sorrows into currency that none remained for herself, and she went **Hollow** while still employed. The Mint keeps her workstation exactly as she left it, which is to say: spotless, and cold.

## On the Fragile Peace

The peace that followed the Nine-Year Silence holds not by treaty but by **mutual indigestion**: each nation still carries the other's assumed griefs, and to take up arms again would mean swallowing one's own dead twice. It is a peace made of swallowed things — tender, heavy, and one bad winter away from coming back up.

*Let no one call it peace. Call it what the Concord calls it: the Long Politeness.*
