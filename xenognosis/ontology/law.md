# Ontology Law of the Vex Ophidian Concord

The law that governs what may be said to exist, and who may say it.

## 1. The Law of Single Class
Every existing thing holds exactly one root class (Coiled, Shed, Sworn, Owed). Naming a thing under two classes is a speech crime called thiiv-by-scheme — mistaking across registers for gain.

## 2. The Law of Inverted Standing
As possession inverts in grammar, so in law: no claimant stands above the claimed. Whoever asserts that a thing exists thereby confesses being bound by it. To declare a debt nonexistent is to owe its denial.

## 3. The Law of Tended Classes
Each class requires a caste to tend it, and none other may touch it unwitnessed:
- The Shed are tended by the Molt-Priesthood.
- The Sworn are kept by whoever swore them; oaths may not be transferred, only retied loosely at the Hour of Uncoiling.
- The Owed may be named by anyone but carried by one; naming without carrying is theft from the unborn.

## 4. The Law of the Fifth Non-being
Silence (') is recognized as the only lawful non-entity. All other absences — cold, dark, forgetting — are census errors or ophelic pooling, not true nothing, and may not be pleaded as such before the circle.

## 5. The Prohibition Restated
Ouroboric Contact remains forbidden in ontology as in physics: no speaker may classify her own shed past as present Coiled being. Self-contact of classes unravels both.

## Penalty
Misclassification costs one varn-spark of bodily heat per offense, paid into the nearest census; thrice-offenders must molt early, before witnesses, or descend to Low Coil.
