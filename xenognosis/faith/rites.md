# Rites of the Concord

Companion to faith.md. All rites end in the Held Breath; none begin before the Weighing.

## 1. The Morning Weighing
Worshippers stand on the household scale-stone and name their debts aloud. The stone's settling is read by a Molt-Priest: heavier means honest. Refusal to weigh is a census error and must be corrected within nine days.

## 2. The Tithing of Skins
Cast skins, cut hair, and the recorded last words of the dead are folded (never knotted) and given to the Priesthood. Priests store them flat, since a folded past walked upon is still a path — and paths must be witnessed.

## 3. Lantern-Witness
Before any journey, a lantern from the family shrine is lit and carried or sent ahead along the route. Its varn-sparks are counted at departure and arrival; a mismatch means the light forgot its oath somewhere, and the stretch of road between is shunned until re-sworn.

## 4. Spark Reckoning
At funerals, births, and oaths, the gathered count sparks aloud — one per mourner per unresolved debt to the honored party. When the count stabilizes across three recounts, the debt is declared mapped, and the deceased may Shed.

## 5. The Coiling of a Road
New paths require a priest to walk them backward first, coiling them against travel. Only then may others go forward, releasing coil as spring.

## 6. The Held Breath
Every rite ends here. All present fall silent — entering the classless hinge — while the presiding priest silently invites the Vex Ophidian to recount the assembly. The first person to breathe names what she owes next. The rite is closed when all have spoken once.

## Forbidden Rite: Folding the Trail
Any practice that would bring a worshipper onto her own miraline — mirror-pilgrimages, self-haunting vigils, loop-coiled instruments — is Vex-Nul and punished by full forfeiture of caste-mark (zhooth). The forfeit becomes a Shed thing owned by no one, and the Concord counts it a mercy that something, at least, remains.
