# The Concord Funeral Rite: The Settling of the Tongue

## Opening Words

*Spoken by the Officiant at the door of the Vault of the Swallowed Hour, the body present, the mourners kneeling:*

**"We gather to spend what cannot be refused. This one has finished carrying. Open the ledger; the account closes tonight."**

## The Spoken Rite

1. The Officiant names the dead three times: once as they were born, once as they lived among us, once as they will be remembered. Each naming is answered by the mourners with *"Kept."*
2. A Regret-Wright opens the dead one's thorn-purse and reads aloud the inventory of stored regret — every Gnarl, every Coilmour — grading each by weight of witness, so that the room hears exactly what this life cost and earned.
3. The mourners speak their unpaid regrets about the dead, one by one. These are drawn fresh at the Lachrymal Mint and pressed as a single **death-thorn**, placed beneath the dead one's tongue. It is the only currency a corpse may hold.
4. The Officiant pronounces the closing formula of the Unswallowing, altered for the occasion: *"It is kept, so they need not carry it. And you, who still breathe: carry yours."*

## The Actions

- The body is cooled in ash-water in the manner of minting, so the dead are pressed like a final thorn.
- The death-thorn is struck against the Vault's lintel once. If it hums, the life is judged fully faced; if silent, the mourners owe a season of Silences until someone produces a regret heavy enough to make it sing.
- The body carries nothing else. No goods, no names, no debts cross over.

## Disposition of Regret-Currency

The estate of the dead settles as follows:

1. **Debts owed by the dead** are paid first from their thorn-purse, heaviest Gnarls first, per the Two-Tongue Handshake: creditors taste the dead one's regrets before accepting.
2. **Debts owed to the dead** do not vanish. They pass to the family line and accrue within the next Serpent's Tithe held in trust.
3. Any remainder is divided thus: one Coilmour to the Officiant, one to the Regret-Wright who read the inventory, and the rest to the Vault itself — for the Concord holds that the dead have no heirs to their sorrow, only custodians.
4. A dead one found Hollow is buried anyway, but the Vault records the emptiness on the lintel beside the struck thorn, and the line owes the difference.

## The Witness's Role

Every Concord funeral requires one **Stranger** — a person unconnected to the dead, paid a single fresh Gnarl for attendance. The Stranger:

- tastes the death-thorn before it is sealed, attesting its grade honestly;
- remembers the inventory aloud afterward, since memory confessed before strangers weighs most and the funeral is the last chance to grade a life upward;
- speaks the final line of the rite, which no kin or official may speak: *"I did not know them, and I attest they were worth carrying."*
- carries away one small regret of the dead, freely given, so that somewhere in the living world the deceased continues to be remembered by someone who never needed to forgive them.

Without the Stranger's attestation, the funeral does not close, and the dead remain — in the eyes of the ledger — merely postponed.
